# Transitions and handoffs

The default is the **hard cut**. It is free, invisible, and works in every piece
ever made. Everything below is a deliberate exception.

## The hierarchy

```
1  hard cut                  default, no cost
2  cut on motion             invisible, reads as one continuous move
3  match cut                 same shape/colour/motion across a shot change
4  carried object            something travels from beat N into beat N+1
5  mask / wipe               a designed reveal, on a change of idea
6  dissolve                  a change of world or time, never a change of shot
7  fade to black             only if the brand earned it, and almost never
```

Every step down costs attention. Spending 1–2 per piece is usually right.

## Cut on motion

The cheapest way to make cutting feel expensive: land the cut **mid-move**. A hand
in motion becomes a different hand in motion; a pan becomes a different pan. The
eye reads continuity where there is none.

Practical rules:

- Find the frame where motion is fastest, not where it settles.
- Cut within 2–3 frames of the motion peak.
- Match the *direction* of movement across the cut, not the subject.
- Never cut into a static frame and out of a static frame — that is two fades with
  extra steps.

## Handoffs

A handoff is what makes a sequence a film. Three reliable forms:

| Form | Mechanic | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| carried object | one element travels through every seam (a card, a cursor, a colour) | the eye tracks it, so the cut is free |
| shape match | a circle in A becomes the counter of a letter in B | the brain sees one continuous form |
| cause and effect | A sets something in motion, B is the consequence, with 1–2 f of overlap | logic covers the seam |

Every beat in the sheet must name its handoff. If a beat has none, it is a slide.

## Masks and wipes

A mask that reveals the next shot in a *meaningful direction* reads as designed;
a random wipe reads as a plugin.

- Reveal along the reading direction (left→right in LTR scripts).
- The mask shape should echo something in the content (a doorway, a bar chart's
  column, a progress edge).
- Keep it ≤ 24 frames. Past that, it is a transition the audience watches instead
  of the content.
- End the mask on a *live* frame of the next shot, never on a still.

## Audio-led transitions

The most underused tool. A change of shot on a sound event costs nothing visually
and lands harder than any wipe:

- Cut picture 1–3 frames before the transient.
- Keep the outgoing audio 2–4 frames into the incoming picture (an L-cut): the ear
  accepts what the eye just left.
- A hole in the music before a drop does more than a riser over it.

## The end

The last two seconds are part of the design.

- End in motion, or on a designed stillness of 0.6–1.4 s. Under 0.6 s the piece
  ends mid-motion; over 1.4 s it ends in a hold that reads as a stalled player.
- The end card / logo arrives by cause — it is what the motion built to, not a
  separate slide appended at the end.
- The final shot should be at most ~25% of the runtime. A long tail is where
  retention dies.
- Never fade to black as a default ending. It says "the file ran out".

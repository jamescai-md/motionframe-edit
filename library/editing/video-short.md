# video-short — a raw recording turned into a polished short

**Licence:** MIT · **Source:** https://github.com/petergyang/video-short · **Absorbed:** 2026-11-07

## What it is
A compact skill (`skills/reference.md`, ~70 rules) for turning one recording into a
publishable vertical short: punch-ins, B-roll, motion graphics, on-screen receipts, and a
frame-by-frame review loop.

## Rules worth keeping
1. **A layout table, chosen before editing:** `a-roll` (you, talking), `punch` (same frame,
   scaled), `split` (you + evidence), `receipt` (the artifact full-screen), `page` (a doc,
   a post, a chart). Every sequence is one of five things, and mixing on purpose is the
   craft.
2. **B-roll must show the thing being said**, at the moment it is said. Decorative B-roll
   is the most common self-inflicted wound in short-form.
3. **Capture receipts, not claims.** If the script says "it made $4,000", the frame shows
   the number.
4. **Review frame by frame at 2× speed before shipping**, then at 1× once, end to end.
5. **QA stills**: export 3–5 frames that prove the important claims actually appear.
6. **Text on screen holds for as long as it takes to read**, ~200 ms per word, minimum.

## What it gets wrong
No design system and no motion doctrine — it assumes you already have taste for typography,
colour and timing. It also says little about audio beyond the basics.

## Where this landed
`edit-supervisor` layout table and receipt rule (1–3) · the two-pass review and QA stills
(4, 5) became part of `gatekeeper`'s delivery checklist and gate G7 · reading-time rule (6)
became a measurable check in `motion-director/references/timing.md`.
